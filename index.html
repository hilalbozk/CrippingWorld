Welcome

Space feels different to each of us.

A familiar corner can bring comfort one day and feel overwhelming the next. Our changing bodies, minds, rhythms and relationships shape how we experience everyday spaces.

Through eight playful invitations, this MA research project explores the spatial knowledge within lived experience : beyond conventional maps, standards and checklists.

Why this research?

Accessibility is often described through fixed standards, but our needs and capacities can change from one moment to another. This research pays attention to what spatial plans may overlook: fatigue and pauses, pleasure and comfort, waiting, care, and the small arrangements that shape everyday life.

Rather than asking you to evaluate spaces against a checklist, the toolkit invites you to show what a place feels like from your own experience. Your contributions may help develop alternative spatial maps, diagrams and speculative design experiments, according to the permissions you choose.

Who can participate?

Anyone interested in exploring their everyday spatial experiences. You do not need design knowledge or special materials. You are welcome to share experiences related to disability, health or your body if they feel relevant to you, but there is no expectation to disclose medical or personal information. Choose one or more games, adapt them, leave them unfinished or return later. Writing, drawing, photos, collage, voice notes and memories are equally welcome.

Who Am I?

A little about you, in your own words. You can answer any questions, use another format, or skip this entirely.

Our identities, bodies, minds, experiences and relationships can shape the spaces we inhabit. Yet no single category or diagnosis can fully describe a person.

If you want, introduce yourself through words, a drawing, a collage or a voice note. Experiences related to disability, health or your body are welcome if you choose to share them, but they are never expected.
