Welcome,

The same space does not always feel the same.

A familiar corner can bring comfort one day and feel overwhelming the next. Our changing bodies, minds, rhythms and relationships shape how we experience everyday spaces.

Through eight playful invitations, this MA research project explores the spatial knowledge within lived experience : beyond conventional maps, standards and checklists.

Why this research?

Accessibility standards are essential, but they cannot fully capture the changing ways in which we experience space. This research pays attention to what spatial plans may overlook: fatigue and pauses, pleasure and comfort, waiting, care, and the small arrangements that shape everyday life.

Rather than asking you to evaluate spaces against a checklist, the toolkit invites you to show what a place feels like from your own experience. Your contributions may help develop alternative spatial maps, diagrams and speculative design experiments, according to the permissions you choose.

Who can participate?

Everyone is welcome to participate. This research particularly centers disabled, chronically ill, neurodivergent experiences and experiences of fluctuating capacities, without requiring anyone to identify with a specific category or disclose a diagnosis. You do not need design knowledge or special materials. You are welcome to share experiences related to disability, health or your body if they feel relevant to you, but there is no expectation to disclose medical or personal information. Choose one or more games, adapt them, leave them unfinished or return later. Writing, drawing, photos, collage, voice notes and memories are equally welcome.

Situating ourselves

A little about me

I am a crip-cyborg design researcher exploring the relationships between bodies, time, space, and care. This research grows partly from my own lived experience of chronic illness and fluctuating bodily needs.

My relationship with everyday spaces is not always the same. It changes with time, emotions, energy, technologies, and relations of care.

My aim is to understand space as something that fluctuates, much like my own body. I embrace cripping as a word of possibility, a way to create, to resist, to hope, and to imagine spaces otherwise.

Rather than assuming that my experiences represent others, I invite your perspectives to expand, question, and reshape this research.

A little about you

Our identities, bodies, minds, experiences, and relationships shape how we inhabit spaces, just as spaces shape our everyday experiences. These relationships can change over time, and no single category or diagnosis can fully describe a person.

If you wish, introduce yourself through words, a drawing, a collage, a voice note, or any other form of expression that feels right for you.

Experiences related to disability, health, or your body are welcome if you choose to share them.

You do not need to introduce or define yourself to take part in the games.
